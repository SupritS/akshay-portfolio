# Infrared Organic Photovoltaics

Organic photovoltaic (OPV) technology has emerged as a promising alternative to conventional silicon-based solar cells due to its lightweight nature, flexibility, solution-processability, and potential for low-cost manufacturing.

This review explored the growing field of infrared organic photovoltaics, focusing on materials, device architectures, operating principles, and future opportunities for energy harvesting technologies.

## Research Motivation

A significant portion of solar radiation lies in the near-infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum. Traditional photovoltaic technologies often exhibit limited utilization of this energy range.

Infrared organic photovoltaics seek to address this challenge by developing active materials capable of harvesting lower-energy photons while maintaining the advantages associated with organic electronic systems.

## Research Objectives

* Review infrared organic photovoltaic technologies
* Examine emerging photoactive materials
* Analyze device architectures
* Evaluate performance limitations
* Identify future research directions
* Explore opportunities for renewable energy harvesting

## Organic Photovoltaic Systems

Organic solar cells utilize semiconducting organic materials to convert solar energy into electrical energy.

Advantages include:

* Lightweight construction
* Mechanical flexibility
* Low-temperature processing
* Large-area fabrication potential
* Reduced manufacturing costs

The development of infrared-active materials further expands the range of usable solar radiation and improves overall energy conversion potential.

## Key Topics Covered

The review examined:

* Organic semiconductor materials
* Infrared-absorbing polymers
* Device engineering strategies
* Charge transport mechanisms
* Efficiency limitations
* Future development pathways
