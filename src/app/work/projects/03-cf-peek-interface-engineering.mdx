# Engineering Interfaces in CF/PEEK Laminates

Carbon fiber reinforced polyether ether ketone (CF/PEEK) composites are among the most promising materials for next-generation aerospace structures due to their exceptional mechanical properties, chemical resistance, and thermal stability.

However, achieving optimal performance requires efficient stress transfer between the carbon fibers and the thermoplastic matrix. The quality of this interface directly influences mechanical strength, durability, and multifunctional behavior.

## Research Motivation

Modern aerospace materials are increasingly expected to perform multiple functions simultaneously. In addition to carrying structural loads, future composite systems must provide capabilities such as:

* Electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding
* Thermal management
* Deicing functionality
* Improved durability
* Lightweight design

This research explores how interface engineering can be leveraged to improve both structural and functional performance within a single composite system.

## Research Objectives

* Improve fiber-matrix interactions
* Enhance mechanical performance
* Increase interlaminar strength
* Introduce multifunctional properties
* Improve deicing efficiency
* Enhance electromagnetic shielding performance

## Interface Engineering Strategy

The study focuses on modifying the interaction between carbon fibers and the PEEK matrix to improve stress transfer and overall laminate performance.

Through controlled interface design, the resulting composite system exhibits improved:

* Mechanical strength
* Damage resistance
* Functional efficiency
* Long-term reliability

## Key Findings

The engineered CF/PEEK laminates demonstrated:

* Enhanced mechanical performance
* Improved deicing behavior
* Increased electromagnetic shielding effectiveness
* Better multifunctional capability compared to conventional laminates

These improvements highlight the importance of interface engineering in transforming structural composites into multifunctional engineering materials.
