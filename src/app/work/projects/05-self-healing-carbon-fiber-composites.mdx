# Self-Healing Carbon Fiber Composites

Conventional carbon fiber composites provide exceptional mechanical performance but often suffer from irreversible damage during service. Microcracks, delamination, and impact-induced defects can accumulate over time, reducing structural reliability and increasing maintenance costs.

My research focuses on developing self-healing carbon fiber composites capable of repairing damage through dynamic covalent chemistry, thereby extending service life and improving sustainability.

## Research Motivation

Future aerospace and transportation structures require materials that can not only withstand demanding environments but also recover from damage without extensive repair procedures.

Self-healing composites offer several advantages:

* Increased structural lifespan
* Reduced maintenance costs
* Improved safety and reliability
* Enhanced sustainability
* Greater damage tolerance

## Research Objectives

* Introduce self-healing functionality into structural composites
* Improve mechanical performance
* Enhance interfacial interactions
* Increase damage resistance
* Develop sustainable composite systems
* Enable repairable engineering structures

## Dual Dynamic Covalent Networks

This research utilizes dual dynamic covalent adaptable networks (CANs) integrated within carbon fiber reinforced composite systems.

Dynamic covalent bonds possess the unique ability to break and reform under appropriate stimuli, enabling:

* Crack repair
* Network rearrangement
* Stress relaxation
* Reprocessing capability
* Extended material lifespan

## Role of Graphene Oxide

Graphene oxide serves as a multifunctional reinforcement within the composite system.

Its incorporation provides:

* Improved mechanical properties
* Enhanced interfacial adhesion
* Better stress transfer
* Improved healing efficiency
* Increased structural robustness

The synergistic interaction between graphene oxide and dynamic covalent chemistry creates a composite system capable of simultaneously achieving high mechanical performance and self-healing functionality.

## Key Findings

The developed composite system demonstrated:

* Enhanced mechanical strength
* Improved toughness
* Effective self-healing capability
* Better interfacial performance
* Increased structural durability

The results highlight the potential of combining nanomaterials with dynamic polymer networks to create next-generation multifunctional composites.
